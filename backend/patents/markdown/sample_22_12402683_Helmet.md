## Abstract

The helmet according to an embodiment of the present invention includes a helmet body; a pad coupled to the inside of the helmet body, and a coupling member that separably couples the helmet body and the pad, wherein the coupling member may include a male coupling member disposed in one of either the inside of the helmet body or the pad, and a female coupling member disposed in the other of either the inside of the helmet body or the pad and into which the male coupling member is inserted and fixed, wherein the male coupling member has a first inclined surface at a portion inserted and fixed into the female coupling member, and the female coupling member has a second inclined surface corresponding to the first inclined surface, and when an external force is applied to the pad in a first direction so that the male coupling member is separated from the female coupling member in a state in which the male coupling member and the female coupling member are coupled, the first inclined surface moves along the second inclined surface, and the pad is separated from the helmet body.

## Description

This application is a U.S. National Phase Application under 35 U.S.C. 371 of International Application No. PCT/KR2022/004942, filed on Apr. 6, 2022, which claims priority to and the benefit of Korean Patent Application No. 10-2021-0045327, filed on Apr. 7, 2021, the disclosures of which are incorporated herein by reference in their entirety.

The present invention relates to a helmet, and more particularly, to a helmet capable of removing a pad coupled to the inside of the helmet in emergency situations.

In the case of a two-wheeled vehicle, there is a very high risk that the shock will be transmitted to a rider in the event of an accident. Accordingly, it is essential for the rider of the two-wheeled vehicle to wear a riding safety helmet, that is, a helmet, to protect his head.

In general, the helmet includes an outer shell that primarily protects the wearer's head from external impact, and a pad that is coupled to the inside of the outer shell. In this way, the pad provided inside the outer shell is in close contact with the head of the wearer to provide a sense of wearing stability and prevent the helmet from being easily peeled off when wearing the helmet.

Meanwhile, when an accident occurs and rescue is required for the wearer, the helmet must be removed from the wearer's head. However, it is difficult to separate the helmet due to the pad closely attached to the wearer's head and secondary injuries occur in the wearer's neck during the process of forcibly separating the helmet.

Therefore, there is a need to develop a helmet that can easily remove the pad in an emergency so that the pad can be stably fixed inside the helmet to provide a comfortable fit while easily separating the helmet from the wearer's head in the event of an accident.

(Priority Document) Patent No. 1995947, “Helmet being capable of removing pad in case of emergency” issued by the Korean Intellectual Property Office on Jul. 3, 2019.

The present invention is to solve the problems of the prior art. The purpose of the present invention is to provide a helmet that is easily separated from the wearer's head in an emergency because the pad that is coupled to the inside and disposed between the outer shell and the wearer's head can be effectively removed by a force having a specific direction.

According to an aspect of the present invention, there is provided a helmet including a helmet body; a pad coupled to the inside of the helmet body; and a coupling member that separably couples the helmet body and the pad, wherein the coupling member may include a male coupling member disposed in one of either the inside of the helmet body or the pad, and a female coupling member disposed in the other of either the inside of the helmet body or the pad and into which the male coupling member is inserted and fixed, wherein the male coupling member has a first inclined surface at a portion inserted and fixed into the female coupling member, and the female coupling member has a second inclined surface corresponding to the first inclined surface, and when an external force is applied to the pad in a first direction so that the male coupling member is separated from the female coupling member in a state in which the male coupling member and the female coupling member are coupled, the first inclined surface moves along the second inclined surface, and the pad is separated from the helmet body.

In this case, the male coupling member may include a base portion fixed to either the inside of the helmet body or the pad; an extension portion that protrudes and extends from one side of the base portion and has the first inclined surface on one side thereof; and an expansion end portion provided at an end of the extension portion in an extension direction, at least a portion thereof extending outward than the extension portion in a direction perpendicular to the extension direction.

In addition, a portion of the extension portion having the first inclined surface may protrude outward than the expansion end portion in a direction perpendicular to the extension direction.

In addition, the helmet may further include a cover plate disposed to cover one surface of either the inside of the helmet body or the pad, and fixing the base portion.

In addition, the cover plate may have a through hole formed in a size that does not pass through the base portion, and wherein the base portion is disposed inside the helmet body or between the pad and the cover plate, and the extension portion extends through the through hole.

In addition, the extension portion may include a first extension portion extending through the through hole, and a second extension portion disposed between the first extension portion and the expansion end portion and having the first inclined surface on one side thereof.

In addition, the helmet may further include a movement prevention member may include a fixing portion fixed to the cover plate, and a movement prevention portion connected to the fixing portion to prevent the movement of the male coupling member by being fastened to the extension portion.

In addition, the second extension portion may include a fastening portion to which the movement prevention member is coupled, and the movement prevention portion is coupled to the fastening portion.

In addition, the female coupling member may further include a body having a receiving space to receive at least a portion of the extension portion and the expansion end portion and having the second inclined surface on one side of the inner wall of the receiving space, and an opening formed in the body in communication with the receiving space.

In addition, the opening may include a first portion provided in a shape surrounding the extension and a second portion connected to the first part and provided in a shape of a slot exposing the second inclined surface.

In addition, the second inclined surface and the second portion may be formed to be wider than the expansion end portion.

In addition, the helmet further includes a pulling portion provided to the pad to be pulled by the external force in the first direction, and wherein the first inclined surface and the second inclined surface may be oriented toward the pulling portion or a virtual point extending in the first direction.

According to the present invention, when an external force is applied in a specific direction according to the interaction between the first inclined surface and the second inclined surface, the pad is easily separated from the inside of the helmet body, thereby making it possible to safely separate the helmet from the wearer's head in an emergency.

Referring to FIGS. <b>1</b> and <b>2</b>, the helmet according to an embodiment of the present invention includes a helmet body <b>100</b>, a pad <b>200</b> coupled to the inside of the helmet body <b>100</b>, and a coupling member <b>300</b> that separably couples the helmet body <b>100</b> and the pad <b>200</b>.

The helmet body <b>100</b> serves to protect the head of the wearer. The helmet body <b>100</b> may be made of a material capable of absorbing impact. For example, the helmet body <b>100</b> may include an outer shell <b>110</b> that is made of a hard synthetic resin or the like and has high strength, and a shock absorber <b>120</b> provided inside the outer shell <b>110</b>. In this case, the shock absorber <b>120</b> may be made of expanded polystyrene (EPS) or the like.

In an embodiment of the present invention, the pad <b>200</b> may be coupled to the inner wall of the shock absorber <b>120</b> by the coupling member <b>300</b>. However, the helmet body <b>100</b> does not necessarily include the shock absorber <b>120</b>, the pad <b>200</b> does not also have to be coupled to the inner wall of the shock absorber <b>120</b>, and may be coupled to any other portion of the interior of the helmet body <b>100</b>.

The helmet body <b>100</b> may have an opening <b>130</b> opened in front thereof to secure the user's view. In addition, the opening <b>130</b> may be opened and closed by the shield <b>140</b> rotatably coupled to the helmet body <b>100</b>.

The pad <b>200</b> is disposed between the inside of the helmet body <b>100</b> and the wearer's head to provide a stable fit to the wearer. In an embodiment of the present invention, the pad <b>200</b> may be detachable coupled to the inside of the helmet body <b>100</b> (for example, the inner wall of the shock absorber <b>120</b>) by the coupling member <b>300</b>. The pad <b>200</b> coupled to the inside of the helmet body <b>100</b> may be in contact with the jaw, cheeks, ears, etc. of the helmet wearer.

The pad <b>200</b> may be formed to have elasticity to perform a buffering action between the wearer's head and the helmet body <b>100</b> and to be deformed according to the shape of the wearer's head. In addition, the cover plate <b>400</b> may be disposed to cover one surface of either the inside of the helmet body <b>100</b> or the pad <b>200</b>. In an embodiment of the present invention, a cover plate <b>400</b> is disposed on one surface (a surface in contact with the helmet body <b>100</b>) of the pad <b>200</b>. That is, the cover plate <b>400</b> may cover one surface of the pad <b>200</b> and be disposed between the inner surface of the helmet body <b>100</b> and one surface of the pad <b>200</b>.

The coupling member <b>300</b> includes a male coupling member <b>310</b> disposed in one of either the inside of the helmet body <b>100</b> or the pad <b>200</b>, and a female coupling member <b>320</b> disposed in the other of either the inside of the helmet body <b>100</b> or the pad <b>200</b> and into which the male coupling member <b>310</b> is inserted and fixed.

The movement prevention member <b>500</b> may include a fixing portion <b>510</b> fixed to the cover plate <b>400</b> and a movement prevention portion <b>520</b> connected to the fixing portion <b>510</b> to prevent the movement of the male coupling member <b>310</b> by being fastened to the extension portion <b>313</b>. The expansion end portion <b>314</b> may have a shape that interferes with the opening <b>323</b> of the female coupling member <b>320</b> when force is not applied in the first direction and is applied in the other direction so that separation between the male coupling member <b>310</b> and the female coupling member <b>320</b> can be made only through a path through which the first inclined surface <b>311</b> of the male coupling member <b>310</b> moves along the second inclined surface <b>321</b> of the female coupling member <b>320</b>.

The expansion end portion <b>314</b> may have a shape that allows its cross-sectional area to be modified by elasticity. Accordingly, when the expansion end portion <b>314</b> passes through the opening <b>323</b> of the female coupling member <b>320</b>, the cross-sectional area thereof is reduced and deformed by elasticity to enter the receiving space S, and the state in which the cross-sectional area thereof is expanded in the receiving space S may be recovered.

In this way, when the first inclined surface <b>311</b> protrudes outward than the expansion end portion <b>314</b>, when an external force is applied in the first direction, the first inclined surface <b>311</b> may smoothly move along the second inclined surface <b>321</b> of the female coupling member <b>320</b> without interference from the expansion end portion <b>314</b>.

FIG. <b>10</b> is a diagram showing the arrangement and orientation of the female coupling member of the coupling member of the helmet according to an embodiment of the present invention.

In one embodiment of the present invention, pads <b>200</b> are disposed on both left and right sides of the inner surface of the helmet body <b>100</b>, respectively. In addition, a plurality of coupling member <b>300</b> are provided on both left and right sides to couple the helmet body <b>100</b> and the pad <b>200</b>. In order to remove the pad <b>200</b>, it is necessary to release the coupling state between the male coupling member <b>310</b> and the female coupling member <b>320</b> of each coupling member <b>300</b>, which means that the first inclined surface <b>311</b> of the male coupling member <b>310</b> of each coupling member <b>300</b> must move along the second inclined surface <b>321</b> of the female coupling member <b>320</b>. Therefore, it is preferable that the first inclined surface <b>311</b> and the second inclined surface <b>321</b> of each coupling member <b>300</b> are oriented so that each of the first inclined surface <b>311</b> can move along the second inclined surface <b>321</b> by a force in a single direction.

Referring to FIG. <b>10</b>, it can be confirmed that the second inclined surface <b>321</b> of each female coupling member <b>320</b> is oriented to face an extension line of an operation point of a force or an operation direction of the force so that a coupling state of a plurality of coupling member <b>300</b> can be simultaneously released by the force (displayed by an arrow) acting downward in the drawing. Of course, the first inclined surface <b>311</b> of each male coupling member <b>310</b> is also disposed to correspond to the orientation of each second inclined surface <b>321</b>.

FIG. <b>11</b> is a diagram showing a method of removing a pad in a helmet during an emergency according to an embodiment of the present invention.

Referring to FIG. <b>11</b>, the helmet according to an embodiment of the present invention may include a pulling portion <b>210</b> provided to the pad <b>200</b> to be pulled by an external force in a first direction. More specifically, the pulling portion <b>210</b> is provided at the lower portion of the pad <b>200</b> so that the pad <b>200</b> can be pulled from the wearer's head toward the body. Here, the force in the first direction applied to cause the first inclined surface <b>311</b> of the male coupling member <b>310</b> of each coupling member <b>300</b> to move along the second inclined surface <b>321</b> of the female coupling member <b>320</b> becomes a force acting from the wearer's head toward the body.

In this way, in an embodiment of the present invention, the first inclined surface <b>311</b> of the male coupling member <b>310</b> and the second inclined surface <b>321</b> of the female coupling member <b>320</b> may be oriented toward the pulling portion <b>210</b> or a virtual point extending in the first direction. Through this, the fastening state of the male coupling member <b>310</b> and the female coupling member <b>320</b> of the plurality of coupling member <b>300</b> can be simultaneously released through one external force. As a result, in an emergency situation, it is possible to easily separate the helmet from the head of the helmet wearer, and to prevent secondary injuries that may occur during the process of severely separating the helmet.

The helmet according to the embodiment of the present invention may be basically applied to a riding helmet for a rider such as a two-wheeled vehicle and a race vehicle.
