## Abstract

Disclosed herein is a switching power supply circuit that includes a switching circuit having an input node connected to an input power supply terminal and an output node connected to an output power supply terminal through an output switch, a feedback circuit configured to feed back information based on a voltage appearing at the output node to the switching circuit, and an activation circuit configured to turn ON the output switch after an elapse of a predetermined time after a voltage appearing at the output node exceeds a predetermined value. The switching circuit is configured to adjust a level of a voltage appearing at the output node based on the information to a predetermined level. The feedback circuit includes an adjustment mechanism configured to adjust a relation between a voltage appearing at the output node and the information.

## Description

The present application claims priority from Japanese Application JP 2022-130976 filed on Aug. 19, 2022, the content to which is hereby incorporated by reference into this application.

The present disclosure relates to a switching power supply circuit and, more particularly, to a switching power supply circuit suitably constituting a switching power supply system by being parallel-connected to one or more other switching power supply circuits.

JP 2003-169471A discloses a switching power supply system constituted by a plurality of parallel-connected switching power supply circuits. The switching power supply system disclosed in JP 2003-169471A makes reactivation timing coincident among the plurality of switching power supply circuits by mutually short-circuiting on/off control terminals of PWM control ICs included in the respective switching power supply circuits.

However, when there is a difference in output voltage among a plurality of parallel-connected switching power supply circuits, a switching power supply circuit having a higher output voltage may be put into an overcurrent state or an overload state. Thus, in the switching power supply system described in JP 2003-169471A, even when the switching power supply circuits are reactivated simultaneously, the overcurrent or overload may occur in the switching power supply circuit having a higher output voltage to result in an immediate stop of switching operation.

It is therefore an object of the present disclosure to provide a switching power supply circuit suitably constituting a switching power supply system by being parallel-connected to one or more other switching power supply circuits.

A switching power supply circuit according to the present disclosure includes a switching circuit whose input node is connected to an input power supply terminal and whose output node is connected to an output power supply terminal through an output switch, a feedback circuit that feeds back information based on a voltage appearing at the output node to the switching circuit, and an activation circuit that turns ON the output switch after the elapse of a predetermined time after a voltage appearing at the output node exceeds a predetermined value. The switching circuit adjusts the level of a voltage appearing at the output node based on the feedback information to a predetermined level, and the feedback circuit includes an adjustment mechanism that can adjust the relation between a voltage appearing at the output node and the feedback information.

FIG. <b>1</b> is a block diagram for explaining the configuration of a switching power supply system <b>10</b> according to an embodiment of the present disclosure.

As illustrated in FIG. <b>1</b>, the switching power supply system <b>10</b> according to the present embodiment has a plurality of parallel-connected switching power supply circuits <b>100</b>. The switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> each have an input power supply terminal <b>101</b> which is supplied with an input voltage Vin, an output power supply terminal <b>102</b> which outputs an output voltage Vout, and an activation control terminal <b>103</b>. The output power supply terminals <b>102</b> included in the respective switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> are mutually short-circuited and connected in common to a load <b>12</b>. The input power supply terminals <b>101</b> included in the respective switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> are connected to different power supplies <b>11</b>. The power supply <b>11</b> may be a power pickup transformer that picks up power by utilizing a magnetic field generated around a high-voltage transmission line. The activation control terminals <b>103</b> included in the respective switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> are also mutually short-circuited.

FIG. <b>2</b> is a circuit diagram of the switching power supply circuit <b>100</b>.

As illustrated in FIG. <b>2</b>, the switching power supply circuit <b>100</b> includes a switching circuit <b>110</b>, a feedback circuit <b>120</b>, an activation circuit <b>130</b>, and an output switch <b>140</b>. The switching circuit <b>110</b> includes a transformer T including a primary winding <b>111</b> and a secondary winding <b>112</b>, a switching element <b>113</b> connected to one end of the primary winding <b>111</b>, and a switching control circuit <b>114</b> for controlling the switching element <b>113</b>. The other end of the primary winding <b>111</b> constitutes an input node N<b>1</b> of the switching circuit <b>110</b> and is connected to the input power supply terminal <b>101</b>. One end of the secondary winding <b>112</b> is connected to an output node N<b>2</b> through a diode <b>116</b>. The output node N<b>2</b> is connected to the output power supply terminal <b>102</b> through the output switch <b>140</b>. The other end of the secondary winding <b>112</b> is connected to ground. A capacitor <b>117</b> is connected between the output node N<b>2</b> and the ground.

The switching control circuit <b>114</b> is constituted by, e.g., a controller IC and has a power supply node N<b>11</b>, a ground node N<b>12</b>, a control node N<b>13</b>, and feedback nodes N<b>14</b> and N<b>15</b>. The switching control circuit <b>114</b> operates with a voltage supplied between the power supply node N<b>11</b> and the ground node N<b>12</b> and controls, based on feedback information F supplied to the feedback node N<b>14</b>, the frequency and duty of a switching signal S output from the control node N<b>13</b> to adjust a voltage appearing at the output node N<b>2</b> to a predetermined level. The switching element <b>113</b> supplied with the switching signal S is constituted by, e.g., an N-channel MOS transistor.

A current flowing in the switching element <b>113</b> is supplied to the feedback node N<b>15</b> of the switching control circuit <b>114</b> through a resistor <b>115</b>. The switching control circuit <b>114</b> monitors the current supplied to the feedback node N<b>15</b>. When determining that the current flowing in the switching element <b>113</b> is an overcurrent, the switching control circuit <b>114</b> fixes the level of the switching signal S to an inactive level (e.g., a ground level) to stop the switching operation of the switching element <b>113</b>. This can prevent breakage of the switching element <b>113</b> due to an overcurrent. Similarly, also when determining that the feedback information F indicates an overload state, the switching control circuit <b>114</b> fixes the level of the switching signal S to an inactive level to stop the switching operation of the switching element <b>113</b>. The switching control circuit <b>114</b> is reactivated after the switching operation is stopped, and the switching operation is resumed after the elapse of a predetermined time.

The feedback circuit <b>120</b> generates feedback information F based on a voltage appearing at the output node N<b>2</b> and feeds back the feedback information to the switching circuit <b>110</b>. A voltage appearing at the output node N<b>2</b> substantially coincides with the output voltage Vout when the output switch <b>140</b> is ON. The feedback circuit <b>120</b> includes fixed resistors <b>121</b> and <b>124</b>, a variable resistor <b>122</b>, a shunt regulator <b>123</b>, a photodiode <b>125</b>, and a phototransistor <b>126</b>. The fixed resistor <b>121</b> and the variable resistor <b>122</b> are connected in series between the output node N<b>2</b> and the anode of the shunt regulator <b>123</b> to constitute a voltage divider circuit, and a voltage at a connection point therebetween is supplied to the reference node of the shunt regulator <b>123</b>. The fixed resistor <b>124</b> and the photodiode <b>125</b> are connected in series between the output node N<b>2</b> and the cathode of the shunt regulator <b>123</b>. The photodiode <b>125</b> and phototransistor <b>126</b> constitute a photocoupler and act to transmit the feedback information F from the secondary side to primary side while maintaining insulation between the primary and secondary sides.

The level of the feedback information F varies depending not only on a voltage appearing at the output node N<b>2</b> but also on the dividing ratio of the voltage divider circuit constituted by the fixed resistor <b>121</b> and variable resistor <b>122</b>. At the design stage, the dividing ratio of the voltage divider circuit is set such that a voltage appearing at the output node N<b>2</b> has a predetermined level (e.g., 24 V); however, the level of a voltage actually appearing at the output node N<b>2</b> may deviate from the predetermined level due to individual difference ascribable to manufacturing variations. The variable resistor <b>122</b> serves as an adjustment mechanism to eliminate such a deviation. By adjusting the resistance value of the variable resistor <b>122</b>, the relation between a voltage appearing at the output node N<b>2</b> and the feedback information F is finely adjusted after manufacturing, whereby the level of a voltage appearing at the output node N<b>2</b> is adjusted to a predetermined level (e.g., 24 V).

The activation circuit <b>130</b> includes fixed resistors <b>131</b>, <b>132</b>, a timer IC <b>133</b>, a capacitor <b>134</b>, and an N-channel MOS transistor <b>135</b>. The fixed resistors <b>131</b> and <b>132</b> are connected in series between the output node N<b>2</b> and the ground to constitute a voltage divider circuit, and a voltage at a connection point therebetween is supplied to an input node N<b>21</b> of the timer IC <b>133</b>. The timer IC <b>133</b> outputs an activation signal A of a high level from an output node N<b>22</b> after the elapse of a predetermined time from when the voltage supplied to the input node N<b>21</b> exceeds a predetermined value. The activation signal A is supplied to the gate electrode of the transistor <b>135</b>. The transistor <b>135</b> is connected between the gate electrode of the output switch <b>140</b> constituted by a P-channel MOS transistor and the ground. Thus, when the activation signal A is activated to a high level, the gate electrode of the P-channel MOS transistor constituting the output switch <b>140</b> is grounded to turn ON the output switch <b>140</b>. That is, the output switch <b>140</b> is turned ON after the elapse of a predetermined time from when a voltage appearing at the output node N<b>2</b> exceeds a predetermined value. Meanwhile, the predetermined time counted by the timer IC <b>133</b> can be adjusted by the capacitance of the capacitor <b>134</b> connected to an adjustment node N<b>23</b>.

The activation signal A is supplied also to the activation control terminal <b>103</b>. As described above, the activation control terminals <b>103</b> are connected in common among the plurality of switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> and, accordingly, after activation of the switching circuits <b>110</b> included in the plurality of respective switching power supply circuits <b>100</b>, the activation signal A that has been activated earliest is supplied in common to the switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> through the corresponding activation control terminals <b>103</b>. As a result, the output switches <b>140</b> included in the respective switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> are turned ON substantially simultaneously. Although the activation signal A indirectly controls the output switch <b>140</b> through the transistor <b>135</b> in the example illustrated in FIG. <b>2</b>, it may directly control the output switch <b>140</b>.

By parallel-connecting the thus configured switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> as illustrated in FIG. <b>1</b>, the switching power supply system <b>10</b> according to the present disclosure can be constituted. In this configuration, the output voltage levels of the respective switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> can be made to substantially coincide with one another by adjusting the resistance values of the variable resistors <b>122</b>, thus preventing a specific switching power supply circuit <b>100</b> from being put into an overload state.

When an overcurrent or an overload occurs in a certain switching power supply circuit <b>100</b>, switching operation is stopped in this switching power supply circuit <b>100</b>, and the output voltage Vout therefrom becomes 0 V. As a result, an overcurrent or an overload occurs also in the remaining switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> and, eventually, switching operation is stopped in all the switching power supply circuits <b>100</b>. Thereafter, in each switching power supply circuit <b>100</b>, the switching control circuit <b>114</b> is reactivated to raise the voltage of the output node N<b>2</b>. Then, the activation signal A is activated in the switching power supply circuit <b>100</b> in which the voltage of the output node N<b>2</b> reaches a predetermined value earliest, and this activation signal A is supplied to the other switching power supply circuits <b>100</b>, whereby all the switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> start power output substantially simultaneously. This prevents a specific switching power supply circuit <b>100</b> from being put into an overcurrent or an overload state upon the activation.

In the example illustrated in FIG. <b>1</b>, the switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> are connected to different power supplies <b>11</b>, so that the levels of the input voltages Vin input to the respective switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> do not always coincide with one another. Even under such a condition, according to the switching power supply system <b>10</b> of the present embodiment, the levels of the output voltages Vout from the respective switching power supply circuits <b>100</b> substantially coincide with one another, thus preventing a specific switching power supply circuit <b>100</b> from being put into an overload state.

FIG. <b>3</b> is a circuit diagram of a switching power supply circuit <b>100</b>A according to a modification.

The switching power supply circuit <b>100</b>A according to the modification illustrated in FIG. <b>3</b> differs from the switching power supply circuit <b>100</b> illustrated in FIG. <b>2</b> in that the activation circuit <b>130</b> is connected to an output node N<b>3</b>. Other basic configurations are the same as those of the switching power supply circuit <b>100</b> illustrated in FIG. <b>2</b>, so the same reference numerals are given to the same elements, and overlapping description will be omitted.

The output node N<b>3</b> is one end of the secondary winding <b>112</b> of the transformer T, i.e., the anode of the diode <b>116</b>. The level of the output node N<b>3</b> is higher than that of the output node N<b>2</b> by a forward direction voltage of the diode <b>116</b>; however, the levels of the output nodes N<b>2</b> and N<b>3</b> are substantially linked to each other, so that the output nodes N<b>2</b> and N<b>3</b> can be substantially equated to each other in terms of operations of the feedback circuit <b>120</b> and activation circuit <b>130</b>. Thus, as exemplified by the switching power supply circuit <b>100</b>A of the modification, the feedback circuit <b>120</b> may be connected to the output node N<b>2</b>, while the activation circuit <b>130</b> may be connected to the output node N<b>3</b>.

The technology according to the present disclosure includes the following configuration examples but not limited thereto.

A switching power supply circuit according to the present disclosure includes a switching circuit whose input node is connected to an input power supply terminal and whose output node is connected to an output power supply terminal through an output switch, a feedback circuit that feeds back information based on a voltage appearing at the output node to the switching circuit, and an activation circuit that turns ON the output switch after the elapse of a predetermined time after a voltage appearing at the output node exceeds a predetermined value. The switching circuit adjusts the level of a voltage appearing at the output node based on the feedback information to a predetermined level, and the feedback circuit includes an adjustment mechanism that can adjust the relation between a voltage appearing at the output node and the feedback information.

According to the present disclosure, using the adjustment mechanism that can adjust the relation between a voltage appearing at the output node and the feedback information allows adjustment of the level of an output voltage output from the output power supply terminal. Thus, in a case where a plurality of switching power supply circuits are parallel-connected to constitute a switching power supply system, it is possible to prevent a specific switching power supply circuit from being preferentially put into an overcurrent state or an overload state.

In the present disclosure, the feedback circuit may include a voltage divider circuit that divides a voltage appearing at the output node, and the adjustment mechanism may be constituted by a variable resistor included in the voltage divider circuit. With this configuration, it is possible to easily adjust the level of an output voltage.

In the present disclosure, the switching circuit may include a transformer including a primary winding connected to the input node and a secondary winding connected to the output node, a switching element connected to the primary winding, and a switching control circuit that controls the switching element. The switching control circuit may stop the switching operation of the switching element when determining that a current flowing in the switching element is an overcurrent. This can eliminate an overcurrent state.

In the present disclosure, the switching control circuit may stop the switching operation of the switching element when determining that the feedback information indicates an overload state. This can eliminate an overload state.

A switching power supply system according to the present disclosure includes a plurality of the above-described switching power supply circuits, wherein the output power supply terminals included in the plurality of respective switching power supply circuits are mutually short-circuited, the activation circuits included in the plurality of respective switching power supply circuits each include an activation control terminal that directly or indirectly controls the output switch, and the activation control terminals included in the plurality of respective switching power supply circuits are mutually short-circuited. With this configuration, it is possible to reactivate the plurality of switching power supply circuits simultaneously.

The switching power supply system according to the present disclosure may further include a plurality of power supplies connected respectively to the input power supply terminals included in the plurality of respective switching power supply circuits. With this configuration, even when there is a difference among the levels of input voltages supplied from the plurality of respective power supplies, it is possible to prevent a specific switching power supply circuit from being preferentially put into an overcurrent state or an overload state.

As described above, according to the present disclosure, there can be provided a switching power supply circuit suitably constituting a switching power supply system by being parallel-connected to one or more other switching power supply circuits.
