## Abstract

A tire valve includes: a valve stem passed through a valve mount hole of a rim and screwed with a nut, a retaining part extending sideways from a proximal end of the valve stem, an electronic device having a connection part that fits onto an outer side of the retaining part, a support ring set on the valve stem, the support ring and a portion of the connection part being sandwiched between the retaining part and an opening edge of the valve mount hole by fastening the nut, and an interference avoidance part formed by cutting off part of an outer circumferential surface of the support ring. The support ring and electronic device include guide parts which slide on each other by fastening the nut thereby causing the support ring and electronic device to approach to guide the support ring and electronic device toward the normal rotational position.

## Description

The present disclosure relates to a tire valve inserted into a valve mount hole extending through the rim of a tire wheel from an inner side of the rim, and screwed with a nut on an outer side.

Tire valves of this type, with an electronic device attached to a proximal end of a valve stem, are commonly known (see, for example, Patent Document 1).

Patent Document 1: JP 2004-276799 A (FIG. <b>2</b> and paragraph)

The above tire valve is desired to be mounted such that the electronic device is disposed in a predetermined normal rotational position around the valve mount hole relative to the tire wheel in consideration of centrifugal force, and the operation of attaching the tire valve to a tire wheel in this way required deft skills. Accordingly, the present disclosure proposes a technique that can facilitate the operation of attaching a tire valve such that an electronic device is set in a normal rotational position relative to a tire wheel.

A tire valve according to a first aspect of the present disclosure made to solve the above problem is a tire valve including: a valve stem passed through a valve mount hole extending through a rim of a tire wheel from an inner side of the rim and screwed with a nut on an outer side of the rim; a retaining part extending sideways from a proximal end of the valve stem; an electronic device having a connection part that fits onto an outer side of the retaining part; a support ring set on the valve stem, the support ring and a portion of the connection part being sandwiched between the retaining part and an opening edge of the valve mount hole when the nut is fastened; an interference avoidance part formed by cutting off part of an outer circumferential surface of the support ring and positioned adjacent to a wheel wall portion of the tire wheel near the valve mount hole; and guide parts provided to the support ring and the electronic device, wherein the guide parts slide on each other when the nut is fastened thereby causing the support ring and the electronic device to approach, to guide the support ring and the electronic device toward a normal rotational position where the support ring and the electronic device are at correct rotational positions relative to each other around the valve stem, when the support ring and the electronic device are displaced from the normal rotational position.

Hereinafter a tire valve <b>10</b>A according to a first embodiment of the present disclosure will be described with reference to FIG. <b>1</b> to FIG. <b>7</b>. As shown in FIG. <b>1</b>, the tire valve <b>10</b>A of this embodiment is configured to include an electronic device <b>50</b> at one end of a valve stem <b>11</b>, and attached to a tire wheel <b>90</b> such that the electronic device <b>50</b> is positioned inside the tire <b>93</b>, with the valve stem <b>11</b> passed through a valve mount hole <b>92</b> of the tire wheel.

The tire wheel <b>90</b> includes a rim forming part <b>95</b>, for example, on an outer side of a disc part <b>94</b>. The rim forming part <b>95</b> is configured to have a tubular part <b>91</b>B fitted and welded to an outer side of the disc part <b>94</b>, for example, with a pair of rims <b>91</b> extending sideways from both ends of the tubular part <b>91</b>B (FIG. <b>1</b> shows only one of the rims <b>91</b>). The rim <b>91</b> has a tire mount part <b>91</b>A at the distal end of a flange part <b>91</b>C that extends from an end portion of the tubular part <b>91</b>B and bends sideways. The valve mount hole <b>92</b> extends through one of the rims <b>91</b> in the flange part <b>91</b>C near the tubular part <b>91</b>B. The flange part <b>91</b>C is slightly tilted outward. As shown in FIG. <b>2</b>, a circumferential portion of the opening edge of the valve mount hole <b>92</b> is positioned in an inner corner between the flange part <b>91</b>C and the tubular part <b>91</b>B, and a notch <b>96</b> having a flat surface parallel to the axial direction of the valve mount hole <b>92</b>, for example, is formed in this inner corner.

In this embodiment, the notch <b>96</b> in the tire wheel <b>90</b> described above corresponds to a “wheel wall portion” in the claims that is adjacent to an interference avoidance part <b>33</b> of a support ring <b>30</b>A to be described later. The “wheel wall portion” adjacent to the interference avoidance part <b>33</b> may not necessarily be formed by cutting off part of the tire wheel <b>90</b> and may be, for example, a portion of the tubular part <b>91</b>B that is not cut off. The tire wheel <b>90</b> may be made of iron or aluminum.

The valve stem <b>11</b> is made of metal and, as shown in FIG. <b>3</b>, configured in a symmetrical shape as a whole, with a cylindrical stem body <b>12</b> and a pair of retaining parts <b>13</b> extending from one end of the stem body to both sides. The valve stem <b>11</b> may be made of resin, and/or asymmetrical. Hereinafter in the description of various parts of the tire valve <b>10</b>A, the axial direction along the center axis of the stem body <b>12</b> shall be referred to as a first direction H<b>1</b>, and the direction in which the pair of retaining parts <b>13</b> extends shall be referred to as a second direction H<b>2</b>. One side in the first direction H<b>1</b> where there are the retaining parts <b>13</b> shall be referred to as a “proximal side”, and the opposite side as a “distal side”.

As shown in FIG. <b>2</b>, the stem body <b>12</b> accommodates a valve core <b>20</b> inside. The valve core <b>20</b> has a structure known as a check valve, which restricts the air from passing from the proximal side to the distal side in a flow passage <b>12</b>R inside the valve stem <b>11</b>, while allowing the air to pass in the opposite direction.

As shown in FIG. <b>3</b>, the stem body <b>12</b> on the outside includes a large-diameter part <b>12</b>A on the proximal side and a small-diameter part <b>12</b>B on the distal side, with a tapered part <b>12</b>C in the middle in the axial direction. The small-diameter part <b>12</b>B has an annular groove <b>12</b>M<b>1</b> in the middle in the axial direction, the distal side from this annular groove <b>12</b>M<b>1</b> being a threaded part <b>12</b>G. A valve cap <b>25</b> (see FIG. <b>1</b>) that covers a distal end opening <b>12</b>K of the stem body <b>12</b> is screwed to this threaded part <b>12</b>G.

The large-diameter part <b>12</b>A also has an annular groove <b>12</b>M<b>2</b> in the middle in the axial direction, the distal side from this annular groove <b>12</b>M<b>2</b> being a threaded part <b>12</b>E. As shown in FIG. <b>2</b>, a nut <b>40</b> for fitting the valve stem <b>11</b> to the tire wheel <b>90</b> is screwed to the threaded part <b>12</b>E. The proximal side from the annular groove <b>12</b>M<b>2</b> of the large-diameter part <b>12</b>A is a fitting part <b>12</b>F, to which the support ring <b>30</b>A, a grommet <b>46</b>, and a washer <b>45</b> are fitted. When the tire valve <b>10</b>A is attached to the rim <b>91</b>, a middle portion of the fitting part <b>12</b>F extends through the valve mount hole <b>92</b> of the rim <b>91</b>, with the washer <b>45</b> abutted on the opening edge on the outer side of the rim <b>91</b> and the support ring <b>30</b>A abutted on the opening edge on the inner side of the rim <b>91</b>, and with the grommet <b>46</b> pressed into the valve mount hole <b>92</b> from the inner side of the rim <b>91</b> and making tight contact with an outer surface of the fitting part <b>12</b>F and an inner surface of the valve mount hole <b>92</b>. The shapes of the support ring <b>30</b>A and grommet <b>46</b> will be described in more detail later.

As shown in FIG. <b>3</b>, the pair of retaining parts <b>13</b> extends from the proximal end of the stem body <b>12</b> to both sides as described above. The pair of retaining parts <b>13</b> on the outside has an arcuate surface <b>13</b>B that is semicircular on the side facing the distal end of the stem body <b>12</b>. The pair of retaining parts <b>13</b> on the outside has a flat surface <b>13</b>A continuous over the pair of retaining parts <b>13</b> on the opposite side from the arcuate surface <b>13</b>B. The flow passage <b>12</b>R inside the stem body <b>12</b> opens at the center in the flat surface <b>13</b>A. Alternatively, the retaining parts <b>13</b> may be columnar so that it has a cylindrical outer surface as a whole.

The electronic device <b>50</b> includes, for example, a wireless circuit and a sensor that detects a condition of the tire <b>93</b>, and wirelessly transmits the detection results of the sensor to a tire monitoring device in the vehicle body (not shown). The tire monitoring device monitors the presence or absence of an abnormality in the condition of the tire <b>93</b> based on the received detection results. Concrete examples of the sensor include a pressure sensor that detects internal pressure of the tire <b>93</b>, a temperature sensor that detects temperature inside the tire <b>93</b>, an acceleration sensor capable of detecting vibration applied to the tire wheel <b>90</b>, and so on. Other sensors may be used. There may be one sensor, or a plurality of sensors.

The housing <b>50</b>H includes a case part <b>51</b> that accommodates the electrical circuit, and a connection part <b>52</b>A for coupling the electronic device <b>50</b> to the valve stem <b>11</b>. The structure of the tire valve <b>10</b>A according to this embodiment has been described above. This tire valve <b>10</b>A is attached to the tire wheel <b>90</b> as described below. The tire valve <b>10</b>A, with the nut <b>40</b> and washer <b>45</b> removed from the valve stem <b>11</b>, is inserted into the valve mount hole <b>92</b> of the tire wheel <b>90</b> with no tire <b>93</b> attached thereto, from the inner side of the rim <b>91</b>. The washer <b>45</b> and nut <b>40</b> are then mounted to the valve stem <b>11</b> protruding from the valve mount hole <b>92</b> to the outer side of the rim <b>91</b>, and the nut <b>40</b> is fastened lightly (i.e., temporarily).

At this time, the annular protrusion <b>46</b>A of the grommet <b>46</b> is received into the valve mount hole <b>92</b> from the inner side of the rim <b>91</b>. One of the interference avoidance parts <b>33</b> of the support ring <b>30</b>A is set adjacent to the notch <b>96</b> of the tire wheel <b>90</b>. Further, the lower side of the electronic device <b>50</b> is set to face the tubular part <b>91</b>B of the tire wheel <b>90</b>. Since the electronic device <b>50</b> is tiltable around the retaining part <b>13</b> of the valve stem <b>11</b> at this stage, it is adjusted to take a prescribed normal tilted attitude. Specifically, in this embodiment, the lower side of the electronic device <b>50</b> is parallel to the rotation center of the tire wheel <b>90</b> in the normal tilted attitude, for example. The electronic device is adjusted to take such an attitude, after which the lower faces of the pair of L-shaped legs <b>51</b>L are abutted on an outer circumferential surface of the tubular part <b>91</b>B of the tire wheel <b>90</b>.

When the nut <b>40</b> is fastened with a prescribed fastening torque (final fastening), the support ring <b>30</b>A and the pair of arcuate grooves <b>54</b> of the electronic device <b>50</b> are sandwiched between the pair of retaining parts <b>13</b> of the valve stem <b>11</b> and the inner face of the rim <b>91</b>, so that the electronic device <b>50</b> is fixed to a certain tilted attitude. The grommet <b>46</b> is compressed between the support ring <b>30</b>A and the rim <b>91</b> to make tight contact with an inner surface of the valve mount hole <b>92</b>, an opening edge of the rim <b>91</b>, an outer surface of the fitting part <b>12</b>F of the valve stem <b>11</b> as shown in FIG. <b>4</b>, so that the valve mount hole <b>92</b> is sealed. The above procedure completes the mounting of the tire valve <b>10</b>A.

When the tire valve <b>10</b>A is passed through the valve mount hole <b>92</b>, before the nut <b>40</b> is fastened, the support ring <b>30</b>A is set to a certain rotational position relative to the valve mount hole <b>92</b> by the interference avoidance parts <b>33</b> and the notch <b>96</b> being adjacent to each other. In this state, however, a situation can arise where the electronic device <b>50</b> is displaced from the normal rotational position relative to the support ring <b>30</b>A. When this is the case, according to the tire valve <b>10</b>A of this embodiment, the electronic device <b>50</b> is guided to the normal rotational position relative to the support ring <b>30</b>A in the process in which the nut <b>40</b> is fastened to the valve stem <b>11</b>, causing the support ring <b>30</b>A to approach the connection part <b>52</b>A of the electronic device <b>50</b>, and in which the pair of guide slopes <b>35</b> of the support ring <b>30</b>A and the pair of tapered guide surfaces <b>57</b> of the electronic device <b>50</b> slide on each other, i.e., the displacement from the normal rotational position is automatically corrected. The tire valve <b>10</b>A of this embodiment has such an automatic alignment function whereby the electronic device <b>50</b> is automatically guided to the normal rotational position in the process in which the tire valve <b>10</b>A is attached to the tire wheel <b>90</b>, so that the operation of attaching the tire valve <b>10</b>A is made easier.

When the support ring <b>30</b>A and electronic device <b>50</b> are largely displaced from each other more than a maximum tolerable misalignment angle (of, for example 30° in this embodiment), below which the support ring <b>30</b>A and the electronic device <b>50</b> can be guided toward the normal rotational position, part of the support ring <b>30</b>A without the guide slopes <b>35</b> abuts on the pair of tapered guide surfaces <b>57</b> of the electronic device <b>50</b> and prohibits the support ring <b>30</b>A and electronic device <b>50</b> from approaching each other. This allows the operator to realize that the support ring <b>30</b>A and electronic device <b>50</b> are largely displaced from the normal rotational position, and prevents a situation where the support ring <b>30</b>A and electronic device <b>50</b> are left misaligned relative to each other.

Since the support ring <b>30</b>A including the pair of interference avoidance parts <b>33</b> and the pair of guide slopes <b>35</b> has a rotation symmetry as a whole, it is easy to put the support ring <b>30</b>A and electronic device <b>50</b> back to the normal rotational position by turning them in suitable directions relative to each other, when the support ring <b>30</b>A and electronic device <b>50</b> are largely displaced from each other more than the maximum tolerable misalignment angle, below which the support ring <b>30</b>A and the electronic device <b>50</b> can be guided to the normal rotational position. The rotation symmetry of the support ring <b>30</b>A allows the electronic device <b>50</b> and support ring <b>30</b>A to be readily set in the normal rotational position when assembling the support ring <b>30</b>A by inserting the valve stem <b>11</b> into the ring, so that the operation of assembling the tire valve <b>10</b>A is also made easier.

Since the electronic device <b>50</b> is tiltable around an axis perpendicular to the axial direction of the valve stem <b>11</b> (which is also the axial direction of the valve mount hole <b>92</b>), the tire valve <b>10</b>A can be fixed to various types of tire wheels <b>90</b> having a valve mount hole <b>92</b> in different positions such that the electronic device <b>50</b> does not interfere with an inner face of the tire wheel <b>90</b> by adjusting the tilted attitude of the electronic device as required.

Since the pair of metal arcuate discs <b>56</b> is embedded in the pair of arcuate grooves <b>54</b> of the resin-made housing <b>50</b>H of the electronic device <b>50</b>, the pair of metal arcuate discs <b>56</b> is held between the pair of retaining parts <b>13</b> of the valve stem <b>11</b> made of metal and the support ring <b>30</b>A made of metal when the electronic device <b>50</b> is fixed in any desired tilted attitude, which provides a good balance in strength and allows the electronic device <b>50</b> to be stably fixed in the desired tilted position.

When the electronic device <b>50</b> is located at the normal rotational position relative to the support ring <b>30</b>A, the pair of tapered guide surfaces <b>57</b> of the electronic device <b>50</b> and the pair of guide slopes <b>35</b> of the support ring <b>30</b>A provided for the automatic alignment function described above face each other with a gap therebetween, which prevents a large force from being applied to the pair of guide slopes <b>35</b> and the pair of tapered guide surfaces <b>57</b> when the nut <b>40</b> is fastened.
