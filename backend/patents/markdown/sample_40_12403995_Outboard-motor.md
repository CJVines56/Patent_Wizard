## Abstract

An outboard motor includes an engine, an upper case, a lower case, a cavitation plate, a transmission, a cooling water passage, a first pump, and a second pump. The transmission includes a drive shaft and a propeller shaft. The cooling water passage is connected to the engine and located in the upper case and the lower case. The first pump is above the cavitation plate. The first pump is connected to the drive shaft and operable to send cooling water to the engine through the cooling water passage. The second pump is drivable by the driving force from the transmission to send the cooling water to the engine through the cooling water passage.

## Description

This application claims priority to Japanese Patent Application No. 2022-062042, filed on Apr. 1, 2022. The contents of that application are incorporated by reference herein in their entirety.

The present invention relates to an outboard motor.

An outboard motor includes a water pump and a cooling water passage for supplying cooling water to the engine. A water intake is provided at the bottom of the outboard motor, and the cooling water passage is connected to the engine and the water intake. The water pump delivers the cooling water to the engine through the cooling water passage.

On the other hand, as disclosed in JP-A-2015-67191, some outboard motors include a main pump and a sub-pump. The sub-pump sends cooling water to the main pump, and the main pump sends the cooling water to the engine. In the outboard motor disclosed in JP-A-2015-67191, the main pump is arranged in the upper case. The main pump is driven by an electric motor. The sub-pump is arranged in the lower case. The sub-pump is driven by a rotation of the drive shaft.

In a case where the main pump is driven by an electric motor as in the above outboard motor, a space is required in the outboard motor to accommodate the electric motor. As a result, the size of the outboard motor is increased.

Preferred embodiments of the present invention efficiently send cooling water to engines of outboard motors by a plurality of pumps while reducing the size of the outboard motors.

An outboard motor according to a preferred embodiment of the present invention includes an engine, an upper case, a lower case, a cavitation plate, a transmission, a cooling water passage, a first pump, and a second pump. The upper case is below the engine. The lower case is below the upper case. The cavitation plate is connected to the lower case. The transmission includes a drive shaft and a propeller shaft. The drive shaft extends downward from the engine and is located in the upper case and the lower case. The propeller shaft is connected to the drive shaft, extends in a front-rear direction of the outboard motor, and is located in the lower case. The cooling water passage is connected to the engine and located in the upper case and the lower case. The first pump is above the cavitation plate. The first pump is connected to the drive shaft and is operable to send cooling water to the engine through the cooling water passage. The second pump is drivable by a driving force from the transmission to send the cooling water to the engine through the cooling water passage.

In an outboard motor according to a preferred embodiment of the present invention, the cooling water is efficiently sent to the engine by the first pump and the second pump. Also, the first pump is connected to the drive shaft and driven by the rotation of the drive shaft. Therefore, the outboard motor is smaller than when the first pump is driven by an electric motor.

Preferred embodiments will be described below with reference to the drawings. FIG. <b>1</b> is a side view of an outboard motor <b>1</b> according to a first preferred embodiment. The outboard motor <b>1</b> includes a bracket <b>2</b>, an engine <b>3</b>, a transmission mechanism <b>4</b>, an engine cowl <b>5</b>, an upper case <b>6</b>, and a lower case <b>7</b>. The outboard motor <b>1</b> is attached to a watercraft via the bracket <b>2</b>.

The engine <b>3</b> generates thrust to propel the watercraft. The engine <b>3</b> includes a crankshaft <b>11</b>. The crankshaft <b>11</b> extends in the vertical direction of the outboard motor <b>1</b>. The transmission mechanism <b>4</b> transmits the driving force of the engine <b>3</b> to a propeller <b>12</b>. The transmission mechanism <b>4</b> includes a drive shaft <b>8</b>, a propeller shaft <b>9</b>, and a shift mechanism <b>10</b>. The drive shaft <b>8</b> is connected to the crankshaft <b>11</b>. The drive shaft <b>8</b> extends in the vertical direction of the outboard motor <b>1</b>. The drive shaft <b>8</b> extends downward from the engine <b>3</b>.

The propeller shaft <b>9</b> extends in a front-rear direction of the outboard motor <b>1</b>. The propeller shaft <b>9</b> is connected to the drive shaft <b>8</b> via the shift mechanism <b>10</b>. The propeller <b>12</b> is attached to the propeller shaft <b>9</b>. The shift mechanism <b>10</b> includes a plurality of gears and a clutch. The shift mechanism <b>10</b> switches the transmission direction of the rotation from the drive shaft <b>8</b> to the propeller shaft <b>9</b>. As a result, the watercraft is switched between forward and reverse.

The engine <b>3</b> is disposed in the engine cowl <b>5</b>. The upper case <b>6</b> is disposed below the engine <b>3</b>. The lower case <b>7</b> is disposed below the upper case <b>6</b>. The drive shaft <b>8</b> is disposed in the upper case <b>6</b> and the lower case <b>7</b>. The propeller shaft <b>9</b> and the shift mechanism <b>10</b> are disposed in the lower case <b>7</b>. Specifically, the lower case <b>7</b> includes a torpedo portion <b>13</b>. The torpedo portion <b>13</b> has an outwardly bulging shape. The propeller shaft <b>9</b> and the shift mechanism <b>10</b> are disposed in the torpedo portion <b>13</b>. The cavitation plate <b>14</b> is connected to the lower case <b>7</b>. The cavitation plate <b>14</b> protrudes rearward from the lower case <b>7</b>.

FIG. <b>2</b> is a block diagram showing a cooling system of the outboard motor <b>1</b>. As shown in FIG. <b>2</b>, the engine <b>3</b> includes a water jacket <b>15</b>. The cooling water flowing through the water jacket <b>15</b> cools the engine <b>3</b>. The outboard motor <b>1</b> includes a cooling water passage <b>16</b> and a drain passage <b>17</b>. The cooling water passage <b>16</b> and the drain passage <b>17</b> are connected to the water jacket <b>15</b> of the engine <b>3</b>. The cooling water passage <b>16</b> and the drain passage <b>17</b> are disposed in the upper case <b>6</b> and the lower case <b>7</b>.

The cooling water is supplied from the outside of the outboard motor <b>1</b> to the water jacket <b>15</b> of the engine <b>3</b> through the cooling water passage <b>16</b>. The cooling water is discharged from the water jacket <b>15</b> to the outside of the outboard motor <b>1</b> through the drain passage <b>17</b> and the drain port <b>18</b>. The drain port <b>18</b> is provided in the lower case <b>7</b>, for example. Alternatively, the drain port <b>18</b> may be provided in the upper case <b>6</b>.

The cooling water passage <b>16</b> includes a water intake <b>21</b>, a first passage <b>22</b>, and a second passage <b>23</b>. The outboard motor <b>1</b> includes a first pump <b>25</b>, a second pump <b>26</b>, and a valve <b>27</b>. The water intake <b>21</b> is provided in the lower case <b>7</b>. The water intake <b>21</b> communicates with the outside of the outboard motor <b>1</b>. The cooling water is drawn into the cooling water passage <b>16</b> from the outside of the outboard motor <b>1</b> through the water intake <b>21</b>.

The first passage <b>22</b> is connected to the water intake <b>21</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> is provided in the first passage <b>22</b>. The second passage <b>23</b> is connected to the water intake <b>21</b>. The second pump <b>26</b> is provided in the second passage <b>23</b>. The valve <b>27</b> is provided in the second passage <b>23</b>. The valve <b>27</b> allows the cooling water to flow from the second passage <b>23</b> to the first passage <b>22</b>. The valve <b>27</b> inhibits the flow of the cooling water from the first passage <b>22</b> to the second passage <b>23</b>.

The first passage <b>22</b> and the second passage <b>23</b> are connected to the water jacket <b>15</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> sends the cooling water to the engine <b>3</b> through the first passage <b>22</b>. The second pump <b>26</b> sends the cooling water to the engine <b>3</b> through the second passage <b>23</b>.

As shown in FIG. <b>1</b>, the first pump <b>25</b> is coaxial with the drive shaft <b>8</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> is connected to the drive shaft <b>8</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> is driven by the rotation of the drive shaft <b>8</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> is disposed below the lower end of the bracket <b>2</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> is disposed in the lower case <b>7</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> is disposed above the cavitation plate <b>14</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> is disposed between the upper end <b>90</b> of the lower case <b>7</b> and the cavitation plate <b>14</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> is disposed above the waterline L<b>1</b> where the watercraft is stopped.

The second pump <b>26</b> is disposed in the lower case <b>7</b>. The second pump <b>26</b> is disposed below the first pump <b>25</b>. The second pump <b>26</b> is disposed below the cavitation plate <b>14</b>. The second pump <b>26</b> is disposed below the waterline L<b>1</b> where the watercraft is stopped. The second pump <b>26</b> is disposed in the torpedo portion <b>13</b>.

The second pump <b>26</b> is connected to the propeller shaft <b>9</b>. The second pump <b>26</b> is coaxial with the propeller shaft <b>9</b>. The second pump <b>26</b> is driven by the rotation of the propeller shaft <b>9</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> is a positive displacement pump such as a gear pump or a vane pump. The second pump <b>26</b> is a non-positive displacement pump such as a centrifugal pump or an axial pump.

In the outboard motor <b>1</b> according to the present preferred embodiment, when the engine <b>3</b> is started, the first pump <b>25</b> is driven by the rotation of the drive shaft <b>8</b>. The first pump <b>25</b> sends the cooling water from the water intake <b>21</b> to the water jacket <b>15</b> of the engine <b>3</b> through the first passage <b>22</b>. Further, the rotation of the propeller shaft <b>9</b> drives the second pump <b>26</b>. Thus, the second pump <b>26</b> sends the cooling water from the water intake <b>21</b> to the water jacket <b>15</b> of the engine <b>3</b> through the second passage <b>23</b>.

In the outboard motor <b>1</b> according to the present preferred embodiment, the cooling water is sent to the engine <b>3</b> by the first pump <b>25</b> and the second pump <b>26</b>. Thus, the cooling water is sent to the engine <b>3</b> efficiently. Also, the first pump <b>25</b> is connected to the drive shaft <b>8</b> and driven by the rotation of the drive shaft <b>8</b>. Therefore, the size of the outboard motor <b>1</b> is reduced compared to when an electric motor for driving the first pump <b>25</b> is provided.
