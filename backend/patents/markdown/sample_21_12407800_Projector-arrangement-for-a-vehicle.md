## Abstract

A projector arrangement for a vehicle includes an attachment mechanism that selectively couples with a rear door of the vehicle. The projector arrangement further includes a video projector coupled with the attachment mechanism. The video projector is configured to project image data in a direction toward the vehicle when the rear door is in an open position. A portable container selectively houses the attachment mechanism and the video projector.

## Description

The present disclosure generally relates to a projector arrangement for a vehicle and, more particularly, to a portable projector arrangement that selectively couples with a rear door of a vehicle.

Conventional vehicles typically include infotainment systems and/or entertainment accessories. These infotainment and entertainment systems have advanced over time to allow consumers to experience media in various ways. For example, conventional vehicles may include flip down display screens for displaying video.

Typically, consumers are required to install these aftermarket display assemblies or purchase separate and incompatible display systems for a given vehicle. In addition, if a projection device is implemented in a vehicle, structures of the vehicle cabin may be required to be rearranged to limit obstruction of the video projection.

According to a first aspect of the present disclosure, a projector arrangement for a vehicle includes an attachment mechanism selectively coupling with a rear door of the vehicle. The projector arrangement further includes a video projector coupled with the attachment mechanism. The video projector is configured to project image data in a direction toward the vehicle when the rear door is in an open position.

Referring generally to FIGS. <b>1</b>-<b>4</b>, the present disclosure relates to a projector arrangement <b>10</b> that may enhance a user experience of viewing cinema or the like in an environment around a vehicle <b>14</b>. The projector arrangement <b>10</b> may be portable via a kit <b>12</b> and be configured to selectively couple with a structure of the vehicle <b>14</b>. For example, the projector arrangement <b>10</b> may be removed from the kit <b>12</b> and mounted to the vehicle <b>14</b>. Video projected from the projector apparatus may be viewed from an exterior <b>16</b> of the vehicle <b>14</b> when a rear door of the vehicle <b>14</b> is in an open state, as will be further described herein. The present projector arrangement <b>10</b> may allow for less cumbersome steps to be performed by users <b>18</b> by, for example, not requiring rearrangement of the vehicle cabin or portions thereof. The portable kit <b>12</b> may be stored in the vehicle <b>14</b> and removed by the user <b>18</b> when the vehicle <b>14</b> is stationary, and the portable kit <b>12</b> may include the attachments required to provide an enhanced experience. For example, the kit <b>12</b> may include various sub-compartments to store the projection equipment, such as audio devices, video devices, stands, panels, screens, and the like, as well as trays or other utensils for holding food items to be consumed by the user <b>18</b>. As will be described further herein, the kit <b>12</b> may also operate as a charging station or an electrical storage device that is capable of being powered or charged via a solar panel disposed on the kit <b>12</b>. In this way, the projector arrangement <b>10</b> of the present disclosure may provide for a more enjoyable viewing experience by limiting incompatibility issues with physical features of the vehicle <b>14</b>, limiting assembly time, and/or requiring little to no rearrangement of the interior of the vehicle <b>14</b>.

Referring now more particularly to FIG. <b>1</b>, the vehicle <b>14</b> includes a rear door <b>20</b> that is pivotable between an open position (as illustrated) and a closed position to selectively cover, or close, a rear opening <b>22</b> of the vehicle <b>14</b>. The vehicle <b>14</b> includes a chassis, or a frame <b>24</b>, that includes an overhead panel <b>26</b> and one or more upright framing features <b>28</b> and lateral framing features <b>30</b> that may define the rear opening <b>22</b>. As will be described further herein, the supports <b>28</b>, <b>30</b> may define one or more slots <b>32</b> for receiving hanging parts that support at least a portion of the projector arrangement <b>10</b>.

Still referring to FIG. <b>1</b>, the rear door <b>20</b> includes an interior surface <b>34</b> that, in the closed position, closes off an interior <b>36</b> of the vehicle <b>14</b>. The rear door <b>20</b> may include one or more handles <b>38</b> that may be held by the user <b>18</b> to control opening or closing of the rear door <b>20</b>. The handles <b>38</b> may be a single handle, a pair of handles <b>38</b> (as illustrated), or more than a pair of handles <b>38</b> that allow the user <b>18</b> to shut the rear door <b>20</b>. Notches <b>40</b> may be formed on the rear door <b>20</b> and extend into the interior surface <b>34</b> to allow user manipulation of the rear door <b>20</b> via the handles <b>38</b> and further define an area for the handles <b>38</b>. It is contemplated that the handles <b>38</b> may be contoured or have a particular curvature or geometry that is configured to mate with at least a portion of the projector arrangement <b>10</b>.

For example, the projector arrangement <b>10</b> may include an attachment mechanism <b>42</b> that selectively couples with the pair of handles <b>38</b> of the rear door <b>20</b> via a pair of clips <b>44</b>. A video projector <b>46</b> may be coupled with the attachment mechanism <b>42</b> to allow the video projector <b>46</b> to project image data in a direction toward the vehicle <b>14</b> when the rear door <b>20</b> is in the open position. The attachment mechanism <b>42</b> may include a pair of elongate members <b>48</b>, or supports, that each extend from one of the pair of clips <b>44</b> toward the video projector <b>46</b>, such that the video projector <b>46</b> may be centrally mounted between the clips <b>44</b>. The clips <b>44</b> and the handles <b>38</b> may form a snap-fit connection, such that the clips <b>44</b> may be dimensioned slightly more narrowly than the handles <b>38</b> to allow the clips <b>44</b> to be secured to the handles <b>38</b> upon the user <b>18</b> applying pressure to the clips <b>44</b>. Similarly, the clips <b>44</b> may be removed by applying a similar or different pressure outwardly from the rear door <b>20</b> to remove the clips <b>44</b> from the handles <b>38</b>. In this way, the video projector <b>46</b> selectively couples with rear door <b>20</b> to allow an attached configuration and a detached configuration of the video projector <b>46</b>.

With continued reference to FIG. <b>1</b>, the projector arrangement <b>10</b> may include a projector screen <b>50</b> that selectively couples with the vehicle <b>14</b> via a plurality of brackets <b>52</b> disposed along a periphery <b>54</b> of the projector screen <b>50</b> when the projector screen <b>50</b> is mounted to the vehicle <b>14</b>. The first hook <b>56</b> may engage a wall, or the frame <b>24</b>, of the vehicle <b>14</b>, and the second hook <b>58</b> may engage or couple with the screen <b>50</b>. The second hook <b>58</b> may be secured with the projector screen <b>50</b> via a plurality of holes <b>60</b> formed along the periphery <b>54</b> of the projector screen <b>50</b>. For example, brackets <b>52</b> along lower parts of the screen <b>50</b> may extend laterally or at an oblique angle to hold tension laterally and/or vertically on the screen <b>50</b>.

Still referring generally to FIG. <b>1</b>, the user <b>18</b> of the projector arrangement <b>10</b> may control the projector <b>46</b> remotely via an interface <b>66</b> on a mobile device <b>64</b> or an interface associated with the vehicle <b>14</b> (e.g., a vehicle human-machine interface (HMI)). That video data may be transmitted by the projector <b>46</b> toward the projector screen <b>50</b> and audio associated with the video data to be presented on an audio communication device, such as vehicle speakers, Bluetooth speakers, or speaker devices further described herein. As will also be further described herein, the projector arrangement <b>10</b> may be coupled with an electrical power source when the subassembly (e.g., the projector <b>46</b> and attachment mechanism <b>42</b>) is attached with the rear door <b>20</b> and/or may be powered from a chargeable power device, such as an electrochemical cell (e.g., a battery).

Inside the container <b>68</b>, for example, a tray <b>78</b>, or various sub-compartments of the cavity <b>74</b> may be employed for housing food items and/or other contents (e.g., 3D glasses) that may enhance a viewing experience.

Referring now to FIGS. <b>2</b>A and <b>2</b>B more generally, a solar panel <b>80</b> may be provided on an exterior surface <b>82</b> of the container <b>68</b>. A pair of clasps <b>84</b> extend from an opposing pair of sidewalls <b>72</b> to allow the user <b>18</b> to manipulate the clasps <b>84</b> to lift the container <b>68</b> and carry the container <b>68</b> to/from the vehicle <b>14</b>. In some examples, the video projector <b>46</b> and/or the speakers <b>86</b> may be charged wirelessly (via, e.g., wireless induction charging), thereby allowing minimal or reduced involvement of the user <b>18</b> with plugging or interconnecting wires from a wall of the container <b>68</b> with the subcomponents of the projector arrangement <b>10</b>.
